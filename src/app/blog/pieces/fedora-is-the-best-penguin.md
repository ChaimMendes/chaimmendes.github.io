Fedora is a Linux-based operating system that refuses to crash. Here is why you should move to Fedora (And ditch Windows while you are at it.)

## A Penguin a Day keeps Lagging away.

Fedora's advantage begins with its penguin ancestry, Linux. It's lighter, faster, and can cut boot times to half, or even less(compared with Windows and Mac), especially on old PCs. Then, Fedora also utilizes a technology called zram, which compresses information on your RAM to make 8Gb run as effectively ~12 GB. Memory is so expensive, thanks to AI, that those extra GB probably make the computer more valuable than when you bought it. 

## It will not crash, ever.

Crashing is a Windows hobby. Windows can, in fact, crash in a wide range of ways, including but not limited to the dreaded blue screen, the dreaded black screen,  the not-so-dreaded-but-just-as-deadly white screen, and a system-wide freeze. In my time on Windows, I have successfully reproduced each of these.

These Screens of Death also come in purple, orange, green(developer releases), and red varieties.

On Linux, I have tried, and it refuses to crash. For example, I took a .blend file that needs ~16 GB of RAM space to open on a 8 GB RAM chip with the paging file disabled. Ideally, this should hang Windows, or at least stick you in a “Not Responding, Please wait while we report this issue to Microsoft.” Loop for 30 minutes. However, Linux will simply isolate the impossible-to-achieve process such that the rest of the UI continues to function, allowing you to close the file whenever you want.

## Which Fedora

Fedora comes in many flavours, but for once, the flagship Workstation is beat by KDE Plasma, which packs a much tighter punch(to your RAM).

The Fedora Workstation is arguably one of the best UIs out there, leaving Windows in the dust, and matching, if not beating macOS. This gnome based set up is gorgeous but also kinda heavy. 

So… KDE Plasma. Unlike its GNOME cousin, KDE Plasma is faster and lighter, and just as immensely customisable. If you were a Windows user, KDE Plasma's UI will still be a steep upgrade, even if not macOS level.

In the past, compromising on UI for performance meant something like XFCE, which was….. yeah… but KDE Plasma doesn't let you feel it.
