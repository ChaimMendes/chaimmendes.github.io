Hubble, along with the new upcoming Nancy Drew, are based on nearly identical, relatively easily manufactured US military spy mirrors. The design of a space telescope is entirely mirror-based, so they look the same. But mirrors, the most visible, aren't the cornerstone of a camera; the focus and sensors are. 

Nancy Grace has an entirely different focal length; that leaves one very confused as to how such a similar body can fit two vastly different focal lenghts. Hubble can zoom in on a star a million light-years away. James Webb can zoom in further, but Nancy Drew simply doesn't zoom. It takes the most un-zoomed, wide-angle images, and its mechanical retina is gigantic, with enormous resolution. In a few years(give or take 3), it will make a detailed map of most of the stars in our galaxy, completing the map of the Milky Way that began with Galileo and the other ancients. 

When we need to observe pockets of antimatter and neutron stars, James Webb will be watching for us, but Nancy Grace will complete the map of all the neutron stars and build a star map like we have only seen in Star Wars.
